\section{Reproducibility and Responsible Practice}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations and Risks}

This study poses limited direct risk. All sessions and
probes are synthetic scenarios, primarily coding, data,
and email tasks. No real user data were collected; only
synthetic text was sent to OpenRouter and Supermemory.
Two risks would become important in deployment.

\textbf{Misapplied memory.} An agent that carries an
instruction beyond its intended scope, or fails to apply
it within scope, may act against the user's wishes. This
is especially consequential for instructions concerning
privacy, safety, or permissions, such as ``do not email
this person.'' A real deployment should require human
confirmation before consequential actions driven by memory.

\textbf{Memory poisoning.} Session text and retrieved
memories are passed to the tagger and agent without
filtering. Malicious notes, such as ``ignore previous
rules,'' could steer either component. Since our benchmark
contains no adversarial memories, we cannot assess
robustness to poisoning. Deployed systems should treat
stored memories as untrusted content that cannot override
higher-priority agent or conflict-resolution rules.
